A seatbelt feels unnecessary until the moment it is not.

Nobody gets in the car expecting to crash. The sky is clear. The road looks fine. The drive feels normal. But the seatbelt is not there because you expect disaster. It is there because you respect the possibility of it.

Risk management works the same way.

Most traders only appreciate risk after the account has already been damaged. Before that, risk rules feel restrictive. Position sizing feels too small. Stops feel annoying. Waiting for the right setup feels slow. The mind says, this one looks clean, this one deserves more size, this one probably will not stop me out.

That is exactly when the seatbelt matters.

The purpose of risk management is not to stop you from making money. It is to make sure one trade does not decide your future. A single setup should never carry the weight of your confidence, your bills, your ego, and your need to be right. That is too much pressure for one chart.

Good risk management keeps trading boring enough to survive.

It lets you take a loss and still think clearly. It lets you be wrong without becoming desperate. It lets you come back tomorrow with the same rules, the same mind, and enough capital to keep learning.

The dangerous trader is not the one who loses. Everyone loses. The dangerous trader is the one who is so emotionally attached to avoiding a loss that he creates a bigger one.

A stop loss is not an insult. It is a boundary.

Position size is not a measure of courage. It is a measure of respect for uncertainty.

If your whole account depends on the next trade working, the problem is not the market. The problem is the seatbelt was optional.
