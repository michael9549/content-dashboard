The First Job Is to Stay in the Game

A climber does not test a rope because falling is expected. The rope is tested because gravity never takes a day off.

Risk management serves the same purpose in trading.

A trader can have skill, experience, and a strong setup, yet uncertainty is always present. No analysis removes it. No winning streak cancels it. The market does not owe the next trade the same result as the last one.

That is why survival comes before profit.

Position size should be chosen from the amount that can be lost responsibly, not from the amount a trader hopes to make. A stop should mark where the trade idea is invalid, not where the pain becomes unbearable. Total exposure should reflect how positions may move together, not merely how many positions are open.

These principles can feel overly cautious during favorable conditions. Seat belts also feel unnecessary on a smooth road. Their value becomes obvious only when conditions change quickly.

Good risk management creates emotional freedom. When the possible loss is acceptable before entry, the trader is less likely to panic, move stops, or stare at every tick. The position has room to follow the plan because its outcome cannot threaten the whole account.

Small risk also preserves the ability to learn. A mistake made at controlled size becomes tuition. The same mistake made with excessive leverage can end the education.

The goal is not to avoid all losses. Avoiding all losses would require avoiding all trades. The goal is to ensure that no single trade, day, or emotional decision has the power to remove the trader from the game.

Before asking how much a trade might make, what would change if the first question became, “What does this cost if it fails?”
