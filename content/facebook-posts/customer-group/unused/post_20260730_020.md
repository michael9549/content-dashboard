**The Coach and the Game Film**

A coach does not watch game film to feel better about the final score.

He watches to see what actually happened. Where the spacing broke down. Where the defense reacted late. Where the decision was right even if the shot missed. Where a good outcome hid a bad process.

Traders need game film.

The scoreboard in trading is the profit and loss. It is important, but it is not enough. A winning trade can be poorly executed. A losing trade can be perfect. If the only review is whether money was made, the trader learns the wrong lessons.

This is how bad habits survive.

A trader chases an entry and wins, so chasing gets rewarded. He moves a stop and price comes back, so moving stops feels smart. He oversizes and hits a big win, so risk starts to look flexible. The account balance may celebrate, but the game film tells a different story.

The market will sometimes pay you for mistakes. That is what makes trading dangerous.

A real review process separates outcome from execution.

Was the setup part of the plan?

Was the entry where it was supposed to be?

Was the risk defined before the trade?

Was the exit planned, or emotional?

Was the trade taken during the right session and market condition?

What would the grade be if the profit and loss were hidden?

That last question matters. If a trade would look foolish without the profit attached, it was probably not a good trade. If a loss would still earn a high grade because the process was clean, it deserves respect instead of frustration.

The goal of review is not self-criticism. It is pattern recognition. Over time, the game film shows where the trader leaks money, where discipline breaks, and where the edge is strongest.

You cannot improve what you refuse to watch.

If your last ten trades were graded on execution instead of outcome, what would your report card say?
