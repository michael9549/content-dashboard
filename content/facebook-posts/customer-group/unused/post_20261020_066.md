A bruise is evidence of impact, not evidence of permanent damage.

A trading loss should be viewed the same way. It records a moment when risk met uncertainty. It does not automatically prove that the strategy is broken or that the trader is incapable.

The danger begins when a normal loss is turned into a personal verdict. Once the mind says, "I cannot be wrong again," the next trade carries more than financial risk. It carries the burden of repairing confidence. That burden makes patience harder, position sizing less rational, and exits more emotional.

Every legitimate trading approach has losing outcomes. The useful distinction is between a good loss and a bad loss. A good loss followed the setup, respected the stop, and stayed within the risk plan. It may hurt, but it is part of the cost of operating. A bad loss came from chasing, oversizing, moving a stop, or ignoring a rule. That loss contains a lesson about execution.

Confusing the two creates unnecessary damage. Punishing a sound process because one trade failed leads to constant strategy changes. Excusing a reckless decision because it happened to recover leads to repeated mistakes. The result matters, but the quality of the decision matters more.

A practical recovery routine helps. Record the facts. Name any rule that was broken. Step away long enough for the physical stress to settle. Then decide whether the next qualified setup can be taken at normal planned risk. This keeps one bruise from becoming a chain of self-inflicted injuries.

Losses do not need to be loved. They need to be understood and contained. A mature trader allows each one to remain the size it was supposed to be, financially and emotionally.
