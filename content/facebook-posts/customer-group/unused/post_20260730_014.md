**The Fisherman and the Splash**

A good fisherman does not cast at every splash.

He knows wind can disturb the water. Small fish can make noise. A branch can fall. Movement does not always mean opportunity, and excitement does not always mean dinner.

Charts are full of splashes.

A candle breaks a level and immediately pulls back. A wick grabs liquidity and reverses. Price accelerates for thirty seconds and makes every impatient trader feel like they are missing the move of the day. The screen lights up, the heart rate changes, and suddenly the plan becomes optional.

This is where many traders lose themselves.

They confuse activity with opportunity. They see movement and assume they need to participate. But the market can move all day without offering your setup. That is one of the hardest truths to accept. There will be days when the chart is busy, other traders are posting wins, and your best trade is no trade.

Patience is not passive. Patience is active restraint.

It is watching price approach your area and waiting for confirmation instead of jumping early. It is letting a missed move go without revenge. It is accepting that being flat is still a position. It is knowing that your job is not to catch every fish in the lake. Your job is to wait for the one your method was built to catch.

The market uses urgency as bait. It makes you feel like this is your only chance. It whispers that the move will leave without you, that you studied too hard to sit on your hands, that a slightly imperfect setup is good enough.

Sometimes it will run without you. Let it.

A trader who cannot miss a move will eventually take every bad one. A trader who can miss a move without emotional damage has freedom. He is no longer controlled by the splash.

The edge is not just knowing where to enter. It is knowing what to ignore.

Are you fishing your setup, or are you casting at every splash?
