Consistency is misunderstood in trading.

Most traders hear the word and think it means winning every day. Green day after green day. Smooth equity curve. No setbacks. No frustration.

That version of consistency does not exist for long.

Real consistency is not consistent outcomes. It is consistent behavior.

A trader can take the same quality setup, with the same risk, at the same time of day, and still get different results. One trade runs clean. One chops around. One stops out immediately. The market controls that part.

The trader controls whether they followed the rules.

This is where most people get trapped. They judge the quality of their decision by the result of one trade. If it wins, they call it smart. If it loses, they start changing the plan. That creates a moving target, and a moving target is impossible to master.

Think about any skilled craft. A carpenter does not throw away the blueprint because one board was warped. A pilot does not rewrite the checklist because there was turbulence. The process exists because conditions are unpredictable.

Trading is the same.

A consistent trader is willing to look repetitive. Same preparation. Same risk limits. Same criteria. Same review process. That repetition is not a weakness. It is the container that lets skill compound.

The danger is that inconsistency often feels productive. Changing indicators feels like research. Switching strategies feels like adaptation. Taking extra trades feels like effort. But if none of it is measured against a stable process, it is usually just motion.

The market does not pay for motion. It pays for execution over a large enough sample size.

One trade means very little. Ten trades say something. One hundred trades reveal the truth.

Consistency is the bridge between knowing what to do and becoming the kind of person who does it when money is moving.

Are you measuring your consistency by your results, or by how often you actually follow your process?
