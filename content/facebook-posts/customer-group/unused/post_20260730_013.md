**The Seatbelt and the Open Road**

Nobody puts on a seatbelt because they expect to crash.

They put it on because they understand that confidence does not cancel physics. A good driver can still hit oil on the road. A careful driver can still get cut off. A familiar route can still produce a surprise.

Risk management in trading is the seatbelt.

Most traders only respect risk after the accident. They size too big because the setup looks clean. They move the stop because the market should not have gone there. They hold through invalidation because they are sure the next candle will fix it. Then the damage happens, and suddenly risk rules seem important again.

That is backwards.

Risk rules are not for the trades you expect to lose. They are for the trades you are most certain will win. Certainty is when traders get careless. Certainty is when they convince themselves that this setup deserves more size, more room, more patience, and fewer rules.

The market does not care how clean the setup looks. It does not care how many confirmations line up. It does not care that you waited all morning and finally got your entry. Any single trade can lose. That is not pessimism. That is the price of admission.

The best traders do not survive because they avoid losses. They survive because no single loss is allowed to become a catastrophe.

A stop loss is not an insult. It is a boundary.

Position size is not a flex. It is a decision about survival.

Walking away from a setup with ugly risk is not weakness. It is professionalism.

The open road will always be tempting. Speed feels good until control is gone. The trader who wears the seatbelt may look boring compared to the one flying down the highway, but boring is underrated when the goal is staying in the game.

If your risk rules only matter after you get hurt, they are not rules. They are regrets.

Where in your trading are you driving without a seatbelt?
