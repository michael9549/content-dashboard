Risk management is the part of trading that feels optional until it becomes the only thing that matters.

When trades are winning, risk can seem boring. Position sizing feels conservative. Stops feel like interruptions. Rules feel like they are slowing down growth. Then one oversized trade, one stubborn hold, or one revenge entry can erase weeks of good work.

The market does not need a trader to be wrong many times. It only needs them to be careless once with too much size.

Good risk management is not pessimism. It is respect. It respects uncertainty. It respects the fact that even a strong setup can fail. It respects the trader's future self enough to make sure one decision does not remove tomorrow's opportunity.

Every trade should answer a simple question before entry: if this does not work, what is the cost?

If that cost is unclear, the trade is not ready. If that cost is too large, the trade is not worth it. If accepting that cost feels emotionally impossible, the size is probably too big.

Risk management turns trading from prediction into participation. The trader no longer needs to be right every time. They need the wins, losses, and sizing to make sense together over a series of trades.

That is a different game.

A trader with poor risk management has to be nearly perfect to survive. A trader with strong risk management can be wrong often and still stay in the game long enough to improve.

The goal is not to avoid losses. Losses are part of the business. The goal is to keep losses ordinary.

An ordinary loss is planned, sized correctly, and emotionally survivable. A dangerous loss is improvised.

Is your risk plan designed for the trade you want, or for the mistake you might actually make?
