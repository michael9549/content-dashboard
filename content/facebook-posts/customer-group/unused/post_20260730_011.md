**The Seed and the Season**

A farmer does not plant seeds on Monday and dig them up on Wednesday to see if they are working.

He waters them. He protects the soil. He watches the weather. He accepts that growth has a rhythm, and that rhythm does not care how impatient he feels.

Trading requires the same kind of patience, but most people treat the market like a vending machine. They put in effort, they press a button, and they expect the result to drop immediately. If it does not, they shake the machine. They change the plan. They add size. They force another trade.

That is not discipline. That is frustration looking for something to do.

A trading plan is a seed. It needs repetition before it can prove anything. One trade cannot validate it. One loss cannot destroy it. Even one great day does not mean the process is strong. The only thing that tells the truth is a large enough sample of trades taken under the same rules.

The hard part is that consistency feels invisible while it is working. It does not give you a dopamine hit every time you follow your rules. Nobody claps when you skip a bad setup. Nobody congratulates you for closing the laptop because your session is over. But those are the moments that separate a trader from someone who is just reacting to candles.

Most blown accounts do not come from a lack of knowledge. They come from digging up the seed too early. A trader tries something for a week, hits a losing streak, decides the strategy is broken, and starts over. Then the cycle repeats with a new indicator, a new time frame, or a new guru.

The market rewards the person who can stay with a process long enough to collect real feedback.

The question is not whether your plan worked today. The question is whether you gave it enough clean execution to tell you the truth.

Are you watering the seed, or are you digging it up every time you feel impatient?
